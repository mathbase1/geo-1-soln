The supplied question

The right-angle mark is at the bottom-right vertex. We use the intended tangencies shown in the picture: the circle touches all three sides. The question asks for the whole triangle’s area, not the area outside the circle.

A shorter second proof

The equal-tangent argument already proved that the two perpendicular sides are x+1 and x+2. Their difference is therefore 1, and the hypotenuse is 3.

Call the base length b and the height h. These are just names for AB and BC.

h−b=1,   b²+h²=9

Expand the square of the difference:

1=(h−b)²=h²+b²−2bh

Substitute the Pythagorean result h²+b²=9:

1=9−2bh  ⇒  2bh=8  ⇒  bh=4

So the triangle’s area is

½bh=½×4=2 square units.

This is only the identity (h−b)²=h²−2bh+b² combined with Pythagoras. It is not Heron’s formula, and it does not require either individual side length.
